# Registration Strategy Justification

The registration of ImageCAS CCTA (contrast-enhanced) atlases to Stanford COCA NCCT (non-contrast) scans requires a specialized approach due to the severe domain shift present between the two modalities. Tissues, particularly within the vasculature and cardiac chambers, display completely disparate Hounsfield Unit (HU) profiles depending on the presence of contrast agents. Therefore, Mattes Mutual Information was selected as the optimization metric rather than Mean Squares or Cross-Correlation. Mutual Information does not assume a direct linear or identity correspondence between voxel intensities; instead, it measures the statistical dependence and shared entropy across the images, correctly aligning the structural borders of the heart and aorta regardless of their internal contrast differences. A multi-resolution framework is simultaneously leveraged (downsampling and smoothing the volumes in sequential passes) to avoid the optimizer settling into local minima that are extremely common in cross-modality cardiac images.

A hybrid transformation strategy (Rigid/Affine followed by B-Spline Deformable) provides the optimal balance of spatial correctness. The initial Affine pass explicitly calculates global translation, rotation, and scaling differences resulting from patient positioning and gross anatomical disparities in the scanner bed. This provides a robust initialization for the subsequent B-Spline Deformable registration. The B-Spline pass is computationally expensive but strictly necessary to capture the complex, localized, non-linear deformations characteristic of the beating heart, varying lung inflation states, and tortuous individual coronary trajectories. While a purely Affine approach would boast significantly faster single-scan computation times, it is inherently incapable of warping the non-rigid myocardium and epicardial borders accurately. Since the ultimate metric relies on capturing small, localized calcifications within a tight 10mm radius of the transformed centerlines, prioritizing topological accuracy via B-Splines heavily justifies the reduction in inference speed.
